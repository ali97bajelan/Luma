# Luma Product Improvement Proposal

**Date:** 18 November 2026  
**Basis:** Source audit in [LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md), revision 73b16d3. The reference reports no runtime observations; recommendations distinguish documented behavior from inference and research hypotheses.

## 1. Product assessment and five consequential opportunities

Luma combines a private life log with lightweight personal planning. Its core journeys are:

- Capture a mood or memory, optionally attaching people, places and photos.
- Review a day through Today, Timeline or Calendar.
- Plan and complete tasks, log habits, and focus on a task.
- Reflect through Weekly Review and retrieve older information.
- Protect and move personal records through App Lock and manual backup.

Its strongest value proposition is **a local, offline-capable record that connects intentions with lived experience**. Tasks explain what someone intended; habits and Focus show effort; moods and memories preserve context. Optional integrations enrich that record without being prerequisites. This follows [“App overview”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#app-overview).

The five most consequential opportunities are:

1. **Make Today a clearer action hub.** Daily actions compete with summaries and historical context; full Tasks and Habits sit under More. This is a navigation and hierarchy inference, not evidence that users cannot find them.
2. **Make preservation understandable.** Manual backups are unencrypted, automatic Android backup is disabled, and App Lock recovery is erase-only. These documented boundaries deserve especially clear choices.
3. **Make capture confidently interruptible.** Several state-saving mechanisms exist, but complete draft recovery is not established for every editor. Verify first, then close demonstrated gaps.
4. **Make system-dependent promises explicit.** A configured reminder is not guaranteed delivery; zero imported data is different from unavailable data. Existing status handling should become clearer at the moment users rely on it.
5. **Turn accumulated information into useful retrieval and reflection.** Search and Weekly Review already exist. Their next improvement should be better entry points, clearer scope and actionable interpretation.

For first-time users, the main hypothesis is conceptual overload. For returning users, it is unnecessary navigation and uncertainty about what needs attention. For users with substantial data, preservation and retrieval become increasingly consequential.

**No P0 is justified by this reference.** It documents important limitations, but no current critical blocker or proven data-loss incident.

Shared requirements for every release: use the existing warm-paper/obsidian design system and shared components; retain offline operation, existing data, privacy gates, callbacks, validation, retries and Undo. ## 3. Detailed proposals

### 1. P1 — Make Today the daily action hub
**Type: Improvement**

- **User and problem:** Returning users need to identify and act on today’s commitments quickly. **Inference:** a mixed dashboard plus a three-row task preview may conceal the size or urgency of their workload.
- **Current evidence:** [“Today”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#today) combines context, summary, habits, tasks and Focus; its task preview defaults to three rows. [“Main and nested navigation”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#main-and-nested-navigation) places full task/habit lists under More.
- **Proposed journey:** Before: scan cards, inspect a short preview, then locate the full list. After: see a clearly labeled action section with task counts, due/overdue context, habit progress and active Focus; open the relevant existing list directly. Explicitly reveal overflow rather than implying three rows are the whole workload.
- **Ranking rationale and estimates:** Broad daily reach makes this first. Impact **Large**, confidence **Medium**, effort **Medium**. Improve hierarchy and access before changing primary navigation.
- **Smallest release:** Reorder existing Today content; add explicit task/habit list links and meaningful task overflow counts. Distinguish overdue, due today and undated tasks in the destination list. **Exclude:** manual “plan my day” selection, recurring tasks, new scheduling models and Calendar habit rows.
- **Dependencies, risks and edges:** Preserve selected-day behavior and date labels. “Due today” must not become a new due date. Keep task-family confirmation, stale-write handling, App Lock and Undo; avoid overwhelming large libraries with expanded dashboard lists.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. Every preview exposes a direct path to the full relevant list; truncated task results disclose additional work.
2. Viewing a past day is unmistakable, with an accessible return-to-today action.
3. Completion and habit logging retain existing confirmation, retry and Undo behavior.

**Success metric / validation:** Time and unassisted success in finding all tasks due today and logging a habit. Compare current and proposed flows with new and returning users, including a large synthetic library.

### 2. P1 — Make backup and recovery safer and clearer
**Type: Improvement**

- **User and problem:** People accumulating personal records need to understand how those records survive device loss and what exported files expose.
- **Current evidence:** [“Backup and restore”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#backup-and-restore) documents manual ZIP archives with integrity checks, no encryption, disabled automatic backup and replacement-style restore. [“App lock and restricted capture”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#app-lock-and-restricted-capture) documents erase-only forgotten-credential recovery. **Inference:** users may confuse app locking with archive confidentiality or recoverability.
- **Proposed journey:** Before: export a portable archive or restore over local data. After: choose an encrypted export, understand password consequences, inspect a validated restore summary, and explicitly acknowledge replacement and exclusions. Explain erase-only lock recovery when configuring the lock.
- **Ranking rationale and estimates:** Lower frequency than Today, but consequences increase with accumulated data. Impact **Large**, confidence **High** in the documented boundaries and **Medium** in user confusion, effort **Large**.
- **Smallest release:** First deliver explicit confidentiality, replacement and recovery explanations; then encrypted archive export/import with password confirmation. Retain legacy archive import and clearly label any unencrypted export option. **Exclude:** accounts, cloud sync, password escrow and automatic uploads.
- **Dependencies, risks and edges:** Requires archive compatibility and recovery verification. Preserve existing running/paused Focus export rules. Handle wrong passwords, corruption, low storage, cancelled document selection and fresh App Lock authorization. A simpler alternative is the disclosure-only release; it improves informed choice but leaves archive exposure unchanged.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. Users can correctly explain whether their exported archive is encrypted and what happens if its password is lost.
2. Restore identifies replacement behavior and portable-data exclusions before final confirmation.
3. Invalid archives, wrong passwords and cancellation leave existing records intact.

**Success metric / validation:** Successful independent export-and-restore rate, plus correct comprehension of confidentiality and recovery. Run round trips on test data, including photos and legacy archives; distinguish archive validation from proof of successful restoration.

### 3. P1 — Establish dependable capture drafts
**Type: Improvement**

- **User and problem:** Frequent capture users get interrupted by app switching, photo selection or Android process termination. Losing a meaningful entry would undermine trust.
- **Current evidence:** [“Back, editor state and external entry points”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#back-editor-state-and-external-entry-points) documents saved Mood state and photo-draft identity, but explicitly does not establish process-death survival for every form. [“Photos and restricted capture”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#photos-and-restricted-capture) describes encrypted restricted drafts. **Hypothesis:** recovery is incomplete or unclear in some ordinary editor paths; this is not a documented failure.
- **Proposed journey:** Before: resume an interrupted editor with recovery guarantees varying by flow. After: return to the recoverable draft with its selected date and attachments; explicitly save or discard it.
- **Ranking rationale and estimates:** Capture is central and interruptions are consequential. Impact **Large**, confidence **Medium**, effort **Medium**, subject to verification. If existing flows already satisfy the contract, narrow this item to feedback and demonstrated gaps.
- **Smallest release:** Verify and align Mood and Memory draft recovery, including clear draft/saved status and discard behavior. **Exclude:** a multi-draft inbox, autosaving edits into published records and draft synchronization.
- **Dependencies, risks and edges:** Respect lock boundaries and photo ownership. Cover process death, locale recreation, picker cancellation, failed saves, storage exhaustion and stale edits. Protected draft content must not appear before authorization.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. A supported interrupted draft restores its entered content, occurrence date and valid attachments in the tested recovery scenarios.
2. Failed saves retain recoverable input and offer retry without duplicate records.
3. Explicit discard removes that draft and its exclusively owned temporary photos.

**Success metric / validation:** Draft recovery success and duplicate/lost-entry incidence in a controlled interruption matrix, followed by observed capture sessions.

### 4. P1 — Explain reminder readiness where reminders are set
**Type: Improvement**

- **User and problem:** Task, habit and Focus users need to know whether a saved reminder can actually notify them.
- **Current evidence:** [“Permissions and capabilities”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#permissions-and-capabilities) already describes blocked/required notification status. [“Reminders”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#reminders) documents quiet hours, inexact alarms, existing notification actions and conditional delivery. **Inference:** settings-level status may be too distant from reminder creation.
- **Proposed journey:** Before: configure a schedule and separately inspect notification settings. After: see “schedule saved” separately from notification access, quiet-hour effects and Android timing limits, with a direct repair action.
- **Ranking rationale and estimates:** Frequent, consequential reliance; existing status machinery limits scope. Impact **Large**, confidence **Medium**, effort **Medium**.
- **Smallest release:** Surface existing readiness state alongside reminder configuration; explain the next eligible time and quiet-hour handling. Offer a user-triggered test notification. **Exclude:** exact-alarm permission, delivery guarantees and additional reminder channels.
- **Dependencies, risks and edges:** Distinguish permission denial, app/channel blocking and scheduling limits. A test being requested or posted must not be labeled “seen.” Preserve generic protected previews, snooze behavior and stale-action rejection.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. A blocked notification state is visible without leaving reminder setup, with an actionable route to repair it.
2. Quiet-hour effects are explained before confirmation.
3. Permission denial preserves the saved schedule without claiming visible delivery.

**Success metric / validation:** Percentage of participants who correctly diagnose a deliberately blocked reminder and repair it. Test denied permissions, blocked channels, quiet hours and device scheduling behavior.

### 5. P1 — Simplify first use and capture terminology
**Type: Simplification**

- **User and problem:** First-time users must distinguish Mood, Memory, habits and tasks before understanding how they fit together. **Hypothesis:** vocabulary and optional fields increase time to first value.
- **Current evidence:** [“Glossary”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#glossary), [“Mood entries”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#mood-entries) and [“Memories and memory markers”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#memories-and-memory-markers) establish different capture models. [“Journal records and unreachable editor”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#journal-records-and-unreachable-editor) confirms the legacy Journal route resolves to Memory. The reference does not establish whether onboarding is currently inadequate.
- **Proposed journey:** Before: interpret feature names and choose a composer. After: encounter short outcome descriptions—record how you feel, write something worth keeping, capture something to do—and complete the minimum required input before adding optional context.
- **Ranking rationale and estimates:** Helps comprehension and repeated capture, but needs first-use research. Impact **Medium**, confidence **Medium**, effort **Medium**.
- **Smallest release:** Improve capture-sheet explanations and relevant empty-state actions; progressively reveal optional editor detail where observation shows overload. Use Memory consistently in reachable creation flows. **Exclude:** a mandatory tutorial, permission requests during onboarding, a new Journal composer and data reclassification.
- **Dependencies, risks and edges:** Keep optional controls discoverable and selections visible. Preserve legacy journal identities and supported actions. Explain “remove memory marker” separately from deleting its source.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. A first-time participant can choose the appropriate existing capture flow without coaching.
2. Mood and Memory remain saveable using only their required inputs.
3. Legacy records retain content, identity and supported access paths.

**Success metric / validation:** Correct composer choice and time to first saved entry in first-use studies across all three languages.

### 6. P1 — Make existing search easier to reach and understand
**Type: Improvement**

- **User and problem:** Users with months of records need retrieval without remembering which browsing destination exposes search.
- **Current evidence:** [“Search”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#search) already provides text, date, tag, people and place filters, pagination, saved criteria, distinct empty states and retries. It is entered from Timeline; indexed field/type breadth is not fully established.
- **Proposed journey:** Before: go to Timeline, open Search and infer its coverage. After: reach the same search from Today and Memory Library, understand supported scope, and see active filters clearly. Task lookup links to the existing task-title search rather than promising universal indexing.
- **Ranking rationale and estimates:** High value as data grows, with modest scope. Impact **Large**, confidence **Medium**, effort **Small**. Ranked below essential daily and preservation work because demand depends on library maturity.
- **Smallest release:** Additional entry points, clear scope text and consistent active-filter/reset controls. **Exclude:** a new index, federated search, semantic search and cloud AI.
- **Dependencies, risks and edges:** Verify actual indexed fields before writing scope copy. Preserve lock filtering, pagination, criteria and result position. Keep empty library, no matches, invalid dates and load failure distinct.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. Each new entry point opens the existing search with predictable criteria.
2. Users can identify and clear active constraints without losing their place unexpectedly.
3. Search communicates supported scope without implying task titles or all private fields are indexed.

**Success metric / validation:** Retrieval success and time for known-item tasks using large synthetic English, French and Persian libraries.

### 7. P2 — Explain habit dates, counts and schedule changes
**Type: Improvement**

- **User and problem:** Habit users, especially after travel or schedule edits, need to know what a count represents and which days an edit changes.
- **Current evidence:** [“Habits”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#habits) documents targets, dated counts, correction/Undo, stored tracking zones and effective-dated schedules. **Inference:** correct but subtle rules can produce surprising outcomes.
- **Proposed journey:** Before: edit a target or correct a log and infer the historical effect. After: see “2 of 3,” the selected logging date, and an explicit effective date before saving schedule changes.
- **Ranking rationale and estimates:** Daily value for a narrower audience; primarily explanation of existing rules. Impact **Medium**, confidence **Medium**, effort **Small**.
- **Smallest release:** Consistent dated progress labels and a schedule-change summary in existing screens. Explain tracking zone when it differs from device context. **Exclude:** streak rewards, automatic historical rewrites and habit rows in Calendar.
- **Dependencies, risks and edges:** Preserve distinctions among unscheduled, paused, archived, partial and target reached. Respect future-log restrictions, DST validation and existing correction paths.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. Every logging/correction surface identifies the affected civil date.
2. Schedule edits identify their effective date and preserve earlier history.
3. Unscheduled or paused days are not presented as failed completion.

**Success metric / validation:** Correct prediction of a schedule edit’s effect and successful correction of an accidental log in travel/date-boundary scenarios.

### 8. P2 — Clarify Focus boundaries, recovery and completion
**Type: Improvement**

- **User and problem:** Focus users need predictable escape and recovery paths, plus clarity about whether a session finishing completes its linked task.
- **Current evidence:** [“Focus sessions”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#focus-sessions) documents in-app navigation gating, emergency exit, recovery-required states, task association and a separate break countdown. It does not block other Android apps. **Inference:** users may overestimate enforcement or misunderstand credited time.
- **Proposed journey:** Before: start a session, encounter a navigation guard or recovery state, then interpret the outcome. After: understand the in-app commitment before starting, see credited versus uncertain time during recovery, and choose the next action after finishing.
- **Ranking rationale and estimates:** Meaningful for Focus users, narrower reach than capture and tasks. Impact **Medium**, confidence **Medium**, effort **Medium**.
- **Smallest release:** Clearer start/recovery explanations and a completion panel linking to the associated task’s existing completion flow, reflection and break. **Exclude:** OS-wide blocking, automatic task completion, a new timer engine and additional permissions.
- **Dependencies, risks and edges:** Task-family confirmation and App Lock remain authoritative. Handle deleted tasks, reboot/clock changes, denied notifications and emergency exit. Avoid claiming precise background alert delivery.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. Users can explain the navigation guard and leave through the established safe path.
2. Recovery explains credited time and uncertainty without silently awarding ambiguous time.
3. Ending Focus never automatically completes its linked task; break time remains uncredited.

**Success metric / validation:** Unassisted recovery success and comprehension of session/task state after simulated interruptions.

### 9. P2 — Turn Weekly Review into a short reflection loop
**Type: Improvement**

- **User and problem:** Returning users need help translating accumulated metrics into a useful adjustment. **Hypothesis:** comparison alone does not consistently produce reflection.
- **Current evidence:** [“Weekly review”](LUMA_APP_REFERENCE.md#weekly-review) already compares equivalent elapsed periods, exposes source records, separates usage coverage from zero and includes habits/Focus. It deliberately excludes entry prose.
- **Proposed journey:** Before: inspect multiple comparisons and independently decide what matters. After: understand the period and coverage, inspect supporting records, then optionally record one observation as a Memory or make one task/habit adjustment.
- **Ranking rationale and estimates:** Supports long-term value, but usefulness depends on sufficient data and user motivation. Impact **Medium**, confidence **Medium**, effort **Medium**.
- **Smallest release:** Clear period/sample explanations and two optional prompts: “What helped?” and “What would you change?” Link to existing capture/edit flows. **Exclude:** AI summaries, causal claims, a new reflection entity and a monthly dashboard.
- **Dependencies, risks and edges:** Preserve week-start/calendar preferences, equivalent-period comparisons and protected sources. Sparse data should invite reflection without presenting missing observations as poor performance. A historical review must not silently misdate a newly created Memory.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. The compared date ranges and available sample/coverage information are understandable.
2. Each suggested action opens an existing workflow with an explicit date.
3. No comparative language claims that a habit, person or activity caused a mood change.

**Success metric / validation:** Percentage of observed reviews producing a user-described useful insight or intentional adjustment; assess through interviews rather than counting prompt clicks alone.

## 4. Now / Next / Later roadmap

| Horizon | Work | Dependency and release logic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Now** | #1 Today hierarchy; #2 backup/recovery explanations; verify and address #3 draft gaps | Establish a runtime baseline first. Clarify existing behavior without waiting for larger data-format changes. |
| **Next** | Complete encrypted backup in #2; #4 reminder readiness; #5 capture simplification; #6 search access | Encryption follows archive/restore verification. Capture simplification follows draft guarantees so shorter flows remain safe. |
| **Later** | #7 habit explanations; #8 Focus journey; #9 guided review | Use findings from daily-use studies. Review should build on dependable capture and retrieval. |

Accessibility, localization, privacy and existing-data verification belong within each release, rather than being postponed to a final phase. “Later” does not justify deferring a critical defect discovered during validation; such evidence would change priority.

## 5. The three improvements to start with

1. **Today’s action hierarchy:** the broadest opportunity to reduce daily effort using existing functionality.
2. **Backup and recovery clarity:** a small first slice addresses documented, consequential boundaries while encrypted backup is prepared.
3. **Capture draft verification and recovery:** protects the central act of recording personal information. Begin with evidence gathering; expand implementation only where actual gaps exist.

These combine a visible daily benefit with protection of the information that gives Luma increasing value.

## 6. Ideas to defer or avoid

- **Accounts, cloud sync and AI interpretation:** introduce privacy, operating-cost and recovery complexity. Local archives, existing search and user-authored reflection are simpler alternatives.
- **A separate Journal composer:** overlaps Memory and adds another capture decision. Preserve historical journal records without resurrecting a parallel workflow by default.
- **Recurring tasks:** genuinely absent, but adds series-editing, occurrence, overdue, reminder and restore semantics. Validate demand and distinguish deadline-driven repetition from existing habits first.
- **More health categories or location acquisition:** steps are the current Health Connect scope. Additional permissions and sensitive data need a demonstrated user outcome.
- **OS-wide Focus blocking:** substantially changes the product promise and platform requirements. Clarify the current in-app guard first.
- **Navigation customization, streak gamification and additional dashboards:** increase decisions or presentation complexity before the current hierarchy is validated.
- **Unrequested resurfacing of old memories:** potentially valuable, but sensitive records can be unwelcome or expose private context. Research consent and control before experimenting.

## 7. Open questions and research needed

1. **Which daily job brings people back?** Observe task-led, habit-led and memory-led use before deciding whether Today’s hierarchy should vary.
2. **What currently happens during interruption?** Exercise each editor through rotation, language recreation, background termination, picker cancellation and failed saves. Record results separately from source evidence.
3. **What do users believe App Lock and backup protect?** Test understanding of archive confidentiality, forgotten credentials, device loss and restore replacement.
4. **Which reminder failures occur on real devices?** Separate blocked access, quiet hours, scheduling delays and stale actions; do not infer reliability from configuration alone.
5. **What do people try to retrieve?** Establish whether existing search scope satisfies common requests before expanding indexing.
6. **Do users understand date boundaries?** Test historical Today capture, task due dates, habit tracking zones and Persian/Gregorian presentation during travel.
7. **Does Review change decisions?** Observe several weekly reviews; distinguish useful reflection from pressure to increase tracking.
8. **Can the essential journeys be completed accessibly?** Run TalkBack, large-text, RTL, keyboard and device-size checks across capture, completion, recovery and restore. Existing tokens and test sources do not establish runtime conformance.

The first research round should include first-time users, returning users and people working with substantial synthetic or explicitly consented existing data. Ranking should change when observed evidence contradicts these estimates.
